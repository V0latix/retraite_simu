# Simulateur de retraite — France

Web app simulating the French pension system at two coupled scales:
- **Macro** — national demography → economy → pension system → yearly balance, under reform scenarios.
- **Micro** (not built yet) — an individual's pension (régime général + AGIRC-ARRCO) computed *inside* a chosen macro context.

The coupling is the point: macro outputs (PASS, point value, indexation, legal age) feed the micro pension calc. Full spec: `docs/cahier-des-charges.md`.

## Stack
React 19 + Vite + TypeScript (strict) + Tailwind v4. Charts: Recharts. Tests: Vitest. Engine runs in a Web Worker. Static hosting on Vercel. No backend.

## Architecture invariants (do not break)
- **Engine is 100% React-free** (`src/engine/`). Pure, deterministic, testable in isolation.
- **Single projection entry point:** `project(state0, hypothesisSet, horizon) => TimeSeries` (`src/engine/project.ts`). Same signature in deterministic and (future) stochastic mode.
- **A scenario is data, not branches.** Reform levers live in `HypothesisSet.policy` as functions of time — never an `if` in the engine.
- **All scale/barème numbers live in data**, never hard-coded in engine logic. Currently `src/data/seed.ts` (parametric placeholder). Phase 0 replaces it with strict versioned INSEE/HMD/COR JSON.

## Observed vs projected (invariant)
Every time-series chart shows **observed data solid, projected data dashed**, with a `frontier()` rule at the boundary. The convention lives in `src/ui/observed.ts` (`mergeObservedProjected`, `frontier`, `PROJECTED_DASH`) + `ObservedProjected.tsx` (legend) — reuse it, never re-improvise per chart. The engine stays untouched: history is display data, loaded from `historical.json` / `historicalPyramid.json` and merged in the UI. The join year appears in both the observed and projected fields so the segments meet (this is also what COR's own workbooks do). Micro/career charts split at `BASE_YEAR` but are labelled *carrière passée / projetée*, never "observé" — the salary is user input even for past years.

## Data status
- **Observed series** (`node scripts/ingest-historical.mjs`): COR rapport annuel juin 2025 workbooks → dépenses/ressources/solde % PIB, cotisants, retraités, PIB (2002-2024, `Obs` rows); ratio 20-64/65+ (1962-2024); réserves du système au 31/12/2024 = 213,8 Md€ ≈ 7,3 % PIB (Tab 2.3), used to anchor the cumulative-balance chart. INSEE POP3 → pyramides observées 1946 (ordonnances créant le régime général) → 2025; champ *France métropolitaine* before 1991, *France* after — stored per year, never spliced. Retirees lag a year behind (holes are `null`).
- **Demography = real INSEE** (Projections 2021-2070): qx/fertility/migration by age 2025-2070, initial 2025 pyramid. The 7 INSEE variant JSONs are still ingested and on disk, but only `central.json` is loaded — the variants are now slider positions (see below). Regenerate via `node scripts/ingest-insee.mjs`. Calibration: our cohort-component reproduces INSEE's 2070 total (~69M vs 68.1M, ~1.3%) and 65+ share (~30%).
- **Extrapolation past 2070** (`BeyondDataPolicy` = hold/trend/converge, §7) stays in the engine (`atYear`, loader.ts) but is no longer a UI choice: every caller takes the `'hold'` default.
- **Hypotheses are sliders, a scenario is a set of slider positions.** `PolicyParams` carries `tfr` / `netMigration` alongside `productivity` / `unemployment`; `applyHypotheses` (`src/data/hypotheses.ts`) rescales the scenario's yearly totals onto them, age/sex profiles preserved, no engine change. `SCENARIO_PRESETS` (`src/data/scenarioPresets.ts`) is the only place a scenario is defined — same pattern as `REFORM_PRESETS`. Three remain: **central** (INSEE/COR), **pragmatique** (observed trends: TFR 1,45, migration +176k, non-emploi 16,6 % = inscrits France Travail pondérés, + risk overlay), **choc-recession** (the only one still differing by *data*, an unemployment shock shape). The old fécondité/EV/migration/COR-productivité variants are slider values now.
- **A turnkey reform is a clean REPLACEMENT, never a layer.** `applyReform(policy, base, preset)` (`src/data/reformLevers.ts`) resets every reform lever to the scenario reference *first*, then applies the preset's delta — so « Réforme 2023 » always prices the 2023 reform, never « 2023 + a pensionCap tried five minutes ago » under that name (which also made `KpiStrip`'s « impact de vos leviers » describe something other than what it named). The taxonomy that makes "every reform lever" decidable is three closed lists in the same file: `REFORM_LEVER_KEYS` (10, the *Leviers de réforme* card) · `HYPOTHESIS_LEVER_KEYS` (4, *Hypothèses*) · `RISK_LEVER_KEYS` (2, *Contexte & risques*). Their union **is** `POLICY_NUM_KEYS` in `share.ts`, which is now derived from them (add a lever to a card and it rides in the URL for free). A reform never touches the other two groups, and `SCENARIO_PRESETS` never touches the reform group — both guarded in `reformLevers.test.ts`. Two consequences: the sliders have two distinct channels in `App.tsx` (`setReformLever` detaches from the selected reform, `setHypothesis` does not — moving fécondité must not blank « Réforme 2023 »), and the *Réinitialiser* button is split per card (the single old one lived under « Leviers de réforme » while also resetting fécondité and migration).
- **A calendar survives a reform that doesn't touch the age.** Third branch of `applyReform`: a preset with its own `schedule` keeps it (a law); one that sets a flat `legalAge`/`requiredQuarters` **without** a calendar drops the inherited one (a proposal — else the calendar swallows the lever, same rule as `buildHypotheses`); one that touches **neither** (a recette, a sous-indexation) leaves the droit en vigueur in place. That last case was the bug: `schedule: preset.schedule` unconditionally abolished the 2023 phase-in for the five calendar-less presets, applying 64 ans from 2025 to générations still at 62-63 — « Suppression de l'abattement de 10 % » (≈ 0,16 pt de PIB) showed **+0,52 pt in 2027**, three times the measure it names. Guarded in `validation.test.ts`.
- **A custom reform is a flat delta, never a calendar** (the same invariant as below, applied to user-built reforms). `extractReformDelta` omits `schedule` *and* every non-deviating lever, so a reform forked from a law whose age slider never moved records nothing about the age — re-applying it therefore keeps the droit en vigueur's phase-in. Saved reforms live in `localStorage` via `src/lib/customReforms.ts` (the project's only storage): versioned schema, `custom:` key prefix so nothing can shadow a built-in key, never-throwing lazy `storage()` accessor (Safari private mode throws, Node has no `localStorage`), per-entry defensive parsing that drops a corrupt entry rather than the file, and every delta value re-clamped to its `LeverSpec` range on the way out. Node-testable by stubbing `Storage` — that is *why* `storage()` is an accessor and not a module-level const.
- **Three URL params carry the reform card**: `m=c` (« Sur mesure » is open — an independent state, because `reformKey === ''` conflates "nothing chosen yet" with "tuning by hand"), `rn=` (a shared custom reform's name, the only part that isn't already in the numeric params), and `cal=0` (the calendar was dropped). `cal` exists because `setP` kept the inherited calendar when only, say, the cotisation slider moved, while `encodeState` never wrote `schedule` — so reloading the URL the app had just written changed the 2025-2031 solde. `decodeState` now rebuilds the policy through `applyReform`, the same function a click uses, so a link and a click can no longer disagree.
- **A law is a calendar keyed by GÉNÉRATION, a proposal is a flat delta.** `PolicyParams.schedule` (`PolicyAnchor[]`, serializable — it crosses postMessage) carries anchors keyed by **année de naissance**, which is how pension law is actually written (2023: 62 → 64 ans, +3 mois par génération, gén. sept. 1961 → 1968). `buildHypotheses` turns them into `cohortPolicy(generation)`; missing générations interpolate, outside the range clamps (`interpByYear` in `loader.ts`, shared with the COR points — `atSchedule` just remaps the key). `policy(year)` keeps only the flat levers. In `project()` the per-year scalar legal age became `effAge[a]` = âge de sortie de la génération `year - a`, so **the calendar is sticky**: whoever liquidated at 62 ans 9 mois stays retired when the age rises, and a lever no longer moves the whole 62-64 stock. That fix alone brought the LFSS-2026 suspension from ≈7 Md€ down to ≈2 Md€ in 2027, against the government's 1,8 Md€. No schedule ⇒ every génération gets the same flat age ⇒ **byte-identical** output (verified over 2025-2070 × 3 scenarios).
- **The 2023 calendar lives in `DEFAULT_POLICY`** (`systemParams.json` `policy.schedule`), not just in the reform template: `buildCorCalibration()` is built on it, so the reference still tracks COR EEC at every horizon while stopping the old nonsense of applying 64 ans in 2025 to générations still at 62-63. Reform templates are deviations *from* that calendar. A flat `legalAge`/`requiredQuarters` passed without a schedule **drops** the inherited one — in `buildHypotheses` (engine callers) and in `setP` (App.tsx) — else the calendar would silently swallow the slider. Other sliders leave it alone.
- **Legal age is fractional and damped.** `retiredWeight()` (`project.ts`) pro-rates the boundary cohort so 62,75 = « 62 ans 9 mois » works; at an integer age it is exactly the old 0/1 step, so the COR calibration is untouched. `legalAgeEffectiveness` (0,29, `systemParams.calibration`, anchored on `legalAgeRef` = 64 so the reference is unshifted) damps the lever: raw, it priced the 2023 reform at ≈59 Md€ at 2030 against a published 10 (Cour des comptes) – 17,7 (étude d'impact) Md€ range. Raised from 0,26 when the per-génération resolution shrank the same gap mechanically. Guarded by `validation.test.ts` (2040 is the binding year, ≈10,9 Md€). Known limit: one linear elasticity can't be right for both a marginal 2-year move and a return to 60.
- **Pension amounts (macro).** `YearResult.avgPension` = pension brute moyenne, euros constants annuels, *before* the COR benefit multiplier (which drives the mass, not the individual amount) but *after* `pensionCap`. Observed counterpart in `historical.pensions` (COR onglet `Rému_pensions`: pension brute moyenne de l'ensemble des retraités 2005-2023, rémunération brute moyenne des cotisants 2002-2024, € 2023/mois). Charted together in the *système* group, both legs rebased with the existing `rebaseFactor` — the model's per-head levels aren't COR's administrative ones. **`pensionCap`** (€/mois) écrête via `pensionDistribution.json`, now the **DREES fiche 05 bracket table** — 44 tranches of 100 € plus one open bucket, each a `ratio` to the table's own mean weighted by a `share` (%), scaled by `avgPensionObservedMonthly` (1 770 €, COR 2023) and drifted with the model's own pension dynamics. Ratios, so the COR calibration is untouched, and `capFactor` is still *not recomputed* when there is no cap (it is 1 by construction — a ulp on `benefits` would shift the COR-calibrated reference; guarded by a byte-identity test in `engine.test.ts`). Two consequences of replacing the old 10-decile table: the champ narrows to *droit direct* (no réversion — the low end thickens, D10/D1 ≈ 16 vs 9, the high end matches within ~5 %), and a high cap costs much more because the old grid squashed the whole top decile onto its mean (4 000 € in 2040: ≈15 Md€ vs ≈6 Md€ before — the old figure was the flagged "à la louche"). Residual ceiling, flagged in the JSON `meta`: the open bucket (1,7 % above ~4 400 €, closed at a Pareto α=3 mean) is still treated as concentrated on one point, so a cap set *inside it* is an order of magnitude, not a costing. Because the table is expressed in ratios to *its own* mean (1 548 €), it is stretched ~14 % onto the model's scale — that is what makes `mean(pensionCurve) === pensionScale`, i.e. what makes the écrêtement ratio legitimate.
- **Two unemployment thermometers, two observed pasts.** `historical.jobseekers` (`node scripts/ingest-jobseekers.mjs <csv>`, Dares STMT séries *brutes*) carries the France Travail DEFM headcounts A→G quarterly **1996T1→today**; F and G, created by the loi plein emploi, are `0` before 2025T1, so the series is not homogeneous across 2025 (+3,5 pts at once — a scope change, not the labour market). `jobseekerRate()` (the Pragmatique's hypothesis, rolling 4 quarters ÷ 2025 actifs, 16,6 %) is unchanged by that history; `jobseekerRateByYear()` derives its **observed** counterpart year by year (1996-2025, each year over its own active population). The chômage chart shows the observed series *of the selected scenario's measure* — BIT for central/choc, France Travail for pragmatique — hence the `scenario` prop on `MacroCharts`: projecting one measure off the other's past would be a lie about what is being counted.
- **`ingest-historical.mjs` merges, never overwrites.** `historical.json` also holds hand-curated blocks the script can't produce (fécondité/migration/chômage observés, inflation INSEE, réserves FRR). It used to clobber them.
- **Precedence rule (do not break):** in the macro view the sliders *are* the scenario, so `runMacro` uses the user's policy. In multi-scenario views (`runCompare`, `runMicro`) one shared policy would flatten every scenario onto the same hypotheses, so the scenario's own preset **overrides** it (`scenarioPolicy()` in the worker); reform levers still come from the user.
- **No life-expectancy slider**: dropping `mortality-low/high` removed the model's strongest sensitivity with no replacement. The JSONs are still on disk — an EV slider means interpolating qx between the three matrices.
- **Finance block calibrated to COR** (Phase 4): constant (real) euros, base-year anchored to the COR June 2025 reference (`corReference.json`), **convention EEC** (« effort de l'État constant » — the economically-meaningful solde used by the Cour des comptes/public debate, which neutralises State balancing transfers) — dépenses ≈13.9% PIB, solde ≈−0.48% PIB (≈−8.7 Md€) in 2025; solde reaches ≈−1.4% PIB in 2070. The COR headline convention EPR (fonction publique équilibrée par construction) would show ≈0 today but converges to the same −1.4% in 2070; EPR≈EEC for observed years, they diverge only in projection. Outputs in % PIB. `systemParams.json` `calibration` block holds the base anchors + `pensionDriftShare` (noria). **Central tracks COR EEC at every horizon** (`corReference.json` points 2025/30/40/50/60/70): `loader.ts` `buildCorCalibration()` runs the central scenario once with the raw taper, then derives a per-year benefit multiplier `depMul` (flattens dépenses to COR's ~14%, killing the old mid-century overshoot to ~15.7%) and an `otherResPct` (pins resources to COR), passed to `project()` via `EconInit.calibration`. The raw single-year taper remains the fallback when no calibration is supplied (`DEFAULT_ECON`, engine tests). Every scenario and reform lever reuses the *same* calibration, so they deviate from COR through their own demography/contributions — sensitivity is preserved (e.g. 2070 solde: central −1.4%, pragmatique −3.1%, TFR 1,2 → −4.7%; +5 pt cotisation → +1.4 pt). The model→COR gap is shown in the Validation view.
- **Micro engine (Phase 3) runs in constant (real) euros.** RG (SAM = 25 best capped at PASS, décote/surcote, proratisation) + AGIRC-ARRCO points. Barèmes in `pensionParams.json` — hand-curated/approximate, flagged. The macro→micro coupling (§5.4) ties the AGIRC-ARRCO point value to each scenario's `dependencySystem` (`couplingSensitivity` k, gentle) so the same career yields a different pension per scenario. Replacement rates land in the realistic range (SMIC ~64%, médian ~53%, cadre ~34%); the per-scenario spread is small for near-2049 retirees (honest) and widens for younger cohorts. HMD skipped: INSEE's 1962-2070 qx covers Phase-5.

## Stochastic mode (Phase 5)
Lee-Carter fitted at build time on INSEE observed qx 1962-2021 (`scripts/ingest-insee.mjs` → `leeCarter.json`: α/β/κ, drift, σ per sex). The generator (`src/engine/scenarios/stochastic.ts`) perturbs the **calibrated central** — qx·exp(β_a·z_t) with z a driftless RW (σ from the fit), plus RW factors on fertility/migration (hand-tuned σ, flagged) — so the median tracks the deterministic central and the fan is data-grounded. `runStochastic` (fanchart.ts) runs `project()` K times → p5/25/50/75/95 bands. `project()` itself is unchanged (invariant held). Fan tab covers solde %PIB, dependency, 65+ share.

## Macro view UX (shared patterns)
Sidebar = sticky (`lg:sticky lg:self-start`, needs `self-start` or the grid item stretches), four cards: **Scénario** (preset picker + horizon) → **Hypothèses** (fécondité, migration, productivité, chômage) → **Leviers de réforme** → **Contexte & risques** (Pragmatique only). Picking a scenario moves the Hypothèses sliders in front of the user; deviating from them shows « — modifié » on the picker and a `Réinitialiser les hypothèses · N` button (no `custom` scenario id). That badge counts **hypotheses only** (`countChangedHypotheses`): a selected reform lives in its own card and must not make the scenario picker claim « Central — modifié ». Per-lever pedagogy lives in `<Hint>` (native `<details>`, `Levers.tsx`) — never inline paragraphs between sliders.

**The Leviers de réforme card has two explicit modes**, a hand-rolled segmented control « Clés en main / Sur mesure » (two `Button`s in a bordered row — `toggle-group` isn't installed, and `Tabs` would imply panels + roving focus; same language as the hand-rolled view tabs). *Clés en main* = the grouped picker (`SelectGroup` « Réformes de référence » / « Mes réformes »), the preset's `source`, its `scheduleSummary`, and a **read-only recap** of `diffReformLevers(policy, base)` — the levers it actually moves, « avant → après ». Rows, not disabled sliders: eleven greyed sliders are ~600px of dead chrome in a 280px column, a slider sitting on its reference value answers nothing, it physically cannot show the *before*, and in a light-brutalist theme the only disabled affordance is `opacity-50`, which reads "broken". *Sur mesure* = the editable sliders (driven by `REFORM_LEVER_SPECS`, one source of truth for both the slider and the recap row), a one-line statement of the calendar's fate (conservé / abandonné + a restore button that puts age, durée **and** calendar back together — restoring the calendar alone would let it override the slider), and the name + Enregistrer + « Mes réformes » list. Switching to *Sur mesure* **is** the fork (`onCustomMode`: blanks `reformKey`, leaves the policy alone — numerically a no-op); « Partir de cette réforme → » is just the named affordance for it. `reforme-2023` selected is the expected empty recap ("c'est la référence"), which is why that state has its own copy. The main column opens on `KpiStrip` (solde/dépenses/dépendance à l'horizon + « impact de vos leviers » = the same scenario re-run with its own preset as baseline, one extra worker). Charts live in five collapsible `<Group>`s — **trajectoire financière** (open by default) / démographie du système / niveau des pensions / hypothèses du scénario / contexte. `Group` is a native `<details>` with an explicit `count` prop (not `React.Children.count`: a `wide` panel still counts once). Chart chrome (`GRID`, `AXIS`, `TOOLTIP`) and legends (`<Legend><Swatch/></Legend>`) are shared — don't re-inline `CartesianGrid`/`stroke`/`<svg><line/>` per chart.

**Two chart traps, both already paid for:**
- A `<Line name>` is what the **tooltip** shows. It said `"Hypothèse INSEE"` on the fertility assumption curve, which lies as soon as the slider leaves INSEE's 1,8 — a hypothesis label must never name a source the user can overrule. Fixed reference lines (`repère : INSEE central (+70k)`) are the legitimate way to show an institutional value.
- **Recharts frames an axis on its bound `dataKey` alone, and an out-of-domain `<ReferenceLine>` is silently dropped** — not clamped, not warned about, just gone. Any chart whose reference lines or marks live outside the data range must compute its domain by hand: the distribution charts' `<XAxis domain>` from `max(last bracket, pensionCap)`, the unemployment chart's `<YAxis>` from `max(0.11, assumption, JOBSEEKER_RATE)`. This is also what the removed box plot fell over: its `<Bar>` carried only `[Q1, Q3]`, so the whiskers overflowed the plot.

**Répartition des pensions** (`dist` in `MacroCharts.tsx`, two panels in *Niveau des pensions*): a **densité** (`ComposedChart`, x = €/mois in `type="number"`, y = % des retraités — aire ensemble + lignes femmes/hommes) and its **cumulée** (`LineChart`, y 0-100 %, « part percevant moins de X »). Both describe the **horizon year only** — the time dimension the old box plot carried through four horizons now lives in the horizon selector, and the `desc` says the curve glides right with the noria without changing shape. The amounts come from `YearResult.pensionCurve` straight out of the engine, never recomputed in the UI, so the curve and the solde always describe the same écrêtement (guarded by `engine.test.ts`); `pensionScale` is exported alongside so the UI can place the bracket *edges* (cumulée) on the same scale. The three curves share one set of brackets — the engine does not project pensions by sex, only the DREES weights differ per column, and the `desc` says so.

**Two masses are drawn as bars, never as points of the curve** (`piled`, `tail`): the retirees rabattus onto a cap (several brackets at the same x) and the open top bucket (1,7 % spread from ~4 400 € to infinity). Neither is a 100 €-wide tranche, so plotting them as one would invent a spike where the data says "a tail". The engine treats both as concentrated on a point — the bars say exactly that. Column normalisation (the fiche's Hommes column sums to 99,6) happens once in `loader.ts`'s `pensionBrackets`, so the JSON stays a verifiable transcription of the source.
